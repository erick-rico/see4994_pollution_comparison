# Research Question – Week 6

> **What large-scale circulation pattern governs extreme air-pollution days in Mexico City, and how does it compare with Hong Kong’s regional meteorology?**

---

## Context

After identifying pollutant–meteorology relationships in Weeks 1–5, Week 6 focuses on the **synoptic-scale drivers** of these pollution peaks.  
The analysis uses **NARR 500 hPa composites** and compares them with existing literature for both **Mexico City** and **Hong Kong**.

---

## Objectives

1. Identify the **dominant atmospheric pattern** (ridge/trough) during high-pollution days (p90 events).  
2. Quantify the mean anomaly (H′) and wind direction over the **MCMA box**.  
3. Link the results with published research on Mexico City’s circulation and stagnation events.  
4. Compare those mechanisms with Hong Kong’s monsoon- and cyclone-related regimes.

---

## Key finding

> Extreme pollution days in CDMX occur under a **500 hPa warm-ridge anomaly** centered over central Mexico, producing **subsidence, weak winds, and atmospheric stability**.  
> This ridge–stagnation regime parallels the blocking-high conditions reported in Díaz-Esteban et al. (2022) and Silva-Quiroz et al. (2019),  
> but differs from Hong Kong, where tropical-cyclone-induced subsidence and monsoonal inflow dominate.
