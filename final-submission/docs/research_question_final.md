## 🎯 Main Research Question  
**What large-scale circulation pattern governs extreme air-pollution days in Mexico City, and how does it compare with Hong Kong’s regional meteorology and health-based air-quality indices?**

This question guided the entire six-week progression of the project and was fully addressed in the final report.

---

## 🧩 Supporting Objectives  
To answer the research question, the study was divided into three nested objectives:

### **1. Pollutant and Index Comparison**  
Analyze pollutant characteristics (PM₁₀, PM₂.₅, O₃, NO₂, SO₂) and compare the structures of Mexico City’s **IAyS** and Hong Kong’s **AQHI**, including their health basis and regulatory context.

### **2. Spatial and Seasonal Analysis of Mexico City**  
Characterize long-term pollutant cycles (2012–2024), compare urban vs. rural behavior, and interpret seasonal variability in relation to the boundary-layer and local meteorology.

### **3. Synoptic Composite Diagnosis (Mexico City)**  
Identify extreme-pollution “event days,” compute 500-hPa geopotential-height anomalies, and characterize the dominant synoptic regime associated with severe air-pollution days.

---

## ✅ Outcome  
All three objectives were successfully achieved, and together they fully answered the main research question. The study revealed:

- A **persistent mid-tropospheric ridge with weak easterly flow** as the dominant synoptic pattern during peak days in Mexico City.  
- A contrast with Hong Kong’s **monsoon and tropical-cyclone-modulated** extreme events.  
- A foundation for my future work, including vertical-structure analysis, HK composites, multi-pollutant thresholds, ENSO-stratified years, and early-warning diagnostics.
