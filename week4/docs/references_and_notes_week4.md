# 📚 References & Notes – Week 4 (Sept 27 – Oct 17 2025)

## Focus
Literature review on **Saharan dust transport, ozone index policy, health exposure studies, and meteorological patterns** influencing air quality in Mexico City.

---

### 🏜️ Saharan Dust
- **Raga et al. (2021)** – ADABBOY project; seasonal dust arrival (June–July) raising background PM.
- **Kutralam-Muniasamy et al. (2021)** – June 2020 dust event; PM₁₀ ↑ 197 µg m⁻³, PM₂․₅ ↑ 94 µg m⁻³; linked to higher COVID-19 mortality.
- *Take-away:* Dust intrusions are recurrent and contribute to summer PM peaks even in the absence of local sources.

### 🌫️ O₃ Averaging Periods (Policy)
- **NOM-172-SEMARNAT-2023** – replaced 8-h O₃ metric with 1-h to improve risk communication.
- Based on **Peng et al. (2013)** (acute O₃ mortality) and conversion ratio **O₃₁ₕ = 1.14 × O₃₈ₕ**.
- *Take-away:* Mexico aligns its index with short-term exposure evidence.

### 😷 Health & Exposure
- **Ugalde-Resano et al. (2022)** – short-term exposure ↑ cardio ED visits (+3–4 % per 10 µg m⁻³ PM₂․₅).  
- **López-Feldman et al. (2021)** – long-term PM₂․₅ exposure ↑ COVID-19 mortality in older adults.  
- **Cromar et al. (2021)** – health-based index combining PM₂․₅, O₃, NO₂; precursor to NOM-172.
- *Take-away:* Air pollution causes both acute and chronic health burdens; indices can translate data into public guidance.

### 🌦️ Weather Patterns
- **Salcido et al. (2019)** – 7 wind + 9 pollutant-flow patterns (local scale).  
- **Silva-Quiroz et al. (2019)** – blocking highs (MAM) trigger O₃ contingencies.  
- **Díaz-Esteban et al. (2022)** – 30-year synoptic WPs; O₃ → spring, PM₂․₅ → winter; La Niña ↑ polluted patterns.
- *Take-away:* Meteorology, especially high-pressure stagnation, is the main driver of seasonal pollution peaks.

---

### 🧩 Integration
Dust → PM baseline ↑ → O₃ episodes → Health impacts → Policy response.  
Meteorology links them all.

---

### 🧠 Core Message
> Meteorology—not only emissions—determines Mexico City’s air-quality extremes. Recognizing these links allows more targeted public-health communication.
